    Design Questions, Answered

    Layered lighting is key. Start with ambient light (a dimmable ceiling fixture or floor lamp), add task lighting (a reading sconce or adjustable table lamp), and finish with accent lighting (LED strips or a small picture light). In small rooms, avoid a single bright overhead source — it flattens the space. Instead, use warm bulbs (2700K) and place fixtures at different heights to create depth.

    Blocking natural circulation paths, pushing all furniture against walls, and ignoring the “triangle” of daily routines. A good plan begins with how you move through the room — not where the sofa looks best. Leave at least 90 cm for main walkways and 45–60 cm between coffee table and sofa. Also, don’t forget vertical space: tall shelving and wall-mounted desks free up floor area.

    Focus on removable, high-impact changes: peel-and-stick backsplash tiles, a new faucet (keep the old one), open shelving with brackets, and a cohesive set of countertop accessories. Lighting is often overlooked — swap out a dated flush mount for a plug-in pendant or a sleek wall sconce. Finally, unify with a rug and a simple color palette of two or three tones.

    Natural stone (honed marble, travertine), solid wood (oak, walnut), lime plaster, and unlacquered brass all develop a patina that adds character. Avoid high-gloss laminates and trend-driven finishes that chip or date quickly. For upholstery, tight-weave linen and wool blends wear gracefully. The key is to let materials be honest — don’t fake marble with porcelain if you can avoid it.
